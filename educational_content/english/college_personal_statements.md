# Writing Personal Statements for College

## What Makes a Strong Personal Statement?

A personal statement isn't just another essay - it's your chance to show colleges who you are beyond grades and test scores. Think of it as a conversation where you're introducing yourself to someone who wants to know what makes you unique.

## Getting Started: Finding Your Story

The hardest part? Figuring out what to write about. Here's the thing - you don't need a dramatic life story. Admissions officers want to see how you think, what you value, and how you've grown.

**Brainstorming prompts:**
- What challenge taught you something important about yourself?
- When did you realize something about the world or other people?
- What activity or interest genuinely excites you, and why?
- What do your friends come to you for help with?

## Structure That Works

**Opening hook (1-2 paragraphs):** Start with a specific moment, not a general statement. Instead of "I've always been interested in science," try "The first time I saw bacteria under a microscope, I couldn't stop thinking about the invisible world all around us."

**Body (3-4 paragraphs):** Connect that moment to a bigger pattern in your life. Show development - how did you pursue this interest? What obstacles did you face? What did you learn?

**Conclusion (1 paragraph):** Connect your story to your future goals and why this college fits into that vision.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ **The resume essay:** Just listing achievements
❌ **The thesaurus attack:** Using complex words to sound smart  
❌ **The victim narrative:** Only focusing on hardship without growth
❌ **The generic essay:** Could apply to anyone

✅ **Do this instead:** Tell a specific story that reveals something authentic about you

## Revision Checklist

- Does this sound like YOUR voice, or could anyone have written it?
- Did you show (with examples) rather than just tell?
- Can someone learn 3 specific things about you from this essay?
- Does every paragraph connect to your main point?
- Would you want to meet the person in this essay?

## Examples of Strong Opening Lines

"I've always been the person who reads the terms and conditions."

"My grandmother doesn't speak English, but she taught me everything about chemistry through her kitchen."

"The first time I failed, I was eight years old and convinced I'd never recover. The second time, I was sixteen and took notes."

Notice how each one is specific and makes you want to know more?

## Final Tips

- Start early - give yourself time to write multiple drafts
- Read it out loud - if it doesn't sound like you talking, revise
- Get feedback from someone who knows you well
- Don't try to be perfect - be authentic
- Remember: they're admitting you, not a character you're playing

Want to practice? Try writing just your opening paragraph first.
